02 · The problem

Shielded pools hide everything, except their edges

Inside the pool you are invisible.
Crossing its edge is public.

Inside a shielded pool

Hidden
  • Sender and receiver
  • Amount
  • Memo

At every boundary

Published, exactly
  • Shield t → pool: inputs, addresses, amount
  • Deshield pool → t: address, amount, time
  • Pool crossing (turnstile): the exact value moved

Why now

Everyone must cross
  • NU6.3 froze Orchard (withdraw-only) at block 3,428,143
  • Every Orchard holder crosses a boundary at least once
  • Wallets don't show users what that reveals
No cryptography is broken. This is what the transaction format reveals at the edges.

05 · How we know we're right

Precision measured, not assumed

Run the rule backwards in time.

Money can't leave before it arrives. Matching each withdrawal against deposits made after it can only find coincidences, which gives the false-link rate on the chain's real amounts.

Tier we reportLinksCoincid.Precision
Non-round, ≤100 blocks2,5351≈99%
Non-round, ≤1 day12819≈85%
Round, ≤10 blocks (small sample)130≈90%
Non-round, >1 day apart447603dropped

More coincidences than links beyond a day, so those links are not reported at all. Round amounts >10 blocks apart: also dropped.

06 · What else the chain says

75.6 days · 584,497 transactions

The pools are busy. The edges are not.

15,668
Round trips: pool → t → pool
Re-shielding the very coins just deshielded links both sides deterministically. 7,370 within 10 blocks; spread over 17,326 addresses, so it is common behaviour, not one actor. 756 left frozen Orchard this way (~101k ZEC) instead of the turnstile.
3.27M ZEC
Orchard → Ironwood migrations, amounts public
24,725 migration transactions. 74% used standard ZIP-318 denominations (good). 3,763 used an amount unique in its window: a fingerprint.
16,510
Address reuse
Shieldings from an address that earlier received a deshielding, across 1,442 addresses. 66.3% come from one address, very likely a service rather than a person.
3 Sep burst

~5,500 shieldings into Ironwood in one day (blocks ~3,470,300–3,470,960); 1,548 of them (~3,000 ZEC) funded from a single transparent address. Visible on the observatory's daily chart.

10 · Why it matters

Turning “leaks are disqualifying” into a product

Privacy fails at the edges. Now it's measured.

For users
Know before you send.
A score, the crowd your amount hides in, and a safer ZIP-318 plan, computed locally. Plain advice: don't deshield and re-shield the same coins; don't withdraw the note you just deposited.
For wallets
Warn at the confirm screen.
The same detectors that produced this report run as an embeddable SDK: refuse round trips, flag reused addresses, default to standard denominations and randomized delays.
For Zcash
Evidence for ZIP-318.
First post-Ironwood measurement of boundary privacy. 74% of migrations already use standard denominations; the other 26%, and 3,763 unique amounts, show what wallets still need to fix.

Every number in this deck is reproducible from public chain data and open code: report.ts + evaluate.ts. Ground truth: our own deliberate testnet mistakes were caught, each leak class reproduced on regtest, mainnet examples spot-checked on an independent explorer.

Open data: summary.json, evaluation.json, examples.json, links.json
